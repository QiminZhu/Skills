# Quiz design

Choose questions aligned to the current objective and level. Mix explanation, prediction, comparison, debugging, and application where relevant; do not force every type into every quiz. Weak areas need reachable next steps rather than a fixed quota of hard questions.

Prefer unaided explanations or performance tasks. For multiple choice, use plausible distractors representing relevant misconceptions, comparable length and specificity, and varied correct-answer positions. Avoid answer-revealing descriptions, formatting, recommended tags, and grammatical clues. Offer only as many credible options as the material supports. Use plain conversation if a question widget adds hints or cannot represent the task fairly.

Prepare an answer criterion grounded in the supplied material or clearly labeled background knowledge. Flag ambiguity rather than penalizing a defensible alternative. In a requested batch quiz, defer feedback until submission unless the learner requests a change of mode.

After submission, identify what was correct, explain the specific gap, and give a fresh opportunity to apply the correction. An incorrect answer alone does not prove a stable misconception. Recognition success is not evidence of free recall or transfer.

Link attempts to stable concept IDs when tracking is active. Schedule only eligible recall items according to scheduling.md. Incorrect or partial recall is Again, not Hard. Do not infer Easy from chat response speed or confidence alone.

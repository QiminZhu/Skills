# Learning strategy policies

Apply these as conditional teaching policies inside Tutor me. They are not separate user-facing modes and must not override an explicit learner request for a direct explanation, a particular source scope, or a chosen Learning/Review/Project mode.

## 1. Goal and capability planning
Use this only when the learner gives a broad capability or career goal rather than a bounded lecture, topic, exercise, or project.

1. Define the target performance in observable terms: what the learner should be able to explain, decide, implement, debug, analyze, or produce.
2. Decompose the target into capabilities and prerequisite relationships. Prefer a small capability graph over a long topic list.
3. Distinguish required capabilities from optional enrichment and avoid inventing requirements for a named program, course, job, or assessment without a source.
4. Establish the learner's current position from stated experience or a small diagnostic when useful; do not force a diagnostic when the learner asks to begin directly.
5. Build a learning sequence that respects prerequisites and includes later application/transfer. Then enter ordinary Learning Mode.

Do not run this planning workflow for a focused request such as “teach slide 12,” “explain BFS,” or “help with this problem.”

## 2. Cognitive chunking
A source hierarchy (Lecture > Topic > Subtopic > Objective) describes content coverage; a cognitive chunk describes concepts or procedures that should become one usable mental unit.

- Group only tightly related objectives whose relationships materially help reasoning or performance.
- Teach component ideas separately when they are new, then explicitly integrate them: name the relationship, show how the pieces interact, and ask the learner to use or explain the combined model.
- Do not create arbitrary chunks merely to reduce item count. Preserve distinct mechanisms that require different decisions.
- When a chunk is integrated, keep the underlying objectives independently auditable for lecture coverage.

## 3. Teach-back
Use teach-back selectively when conceptual understanding or explanation is an objective. Ask the learner to explain the idea in simple language, preferably without repeating the formal definition or formula. Evaluate the relationships and causal/mechanistic content, not eloquence. Identify the smallest missing or incorrect relationship and follow with application when appropriate. Do not require teach-back for every procedural step or when it would add little evidence.

## 4. Bottleneck-targeted deliberate practice
After enough evidence identifies a specific performance bottleneck, target that bottleneck rather than assigning generic more-practice repetitions.

- Describe the bottleneck at the smallest useful level: concept, procedure, strategy selection, representation/setup, debugging, or prerequisite.
- Construct a short practice item that isolates it without stripping away the decision the learner needs to learn.
- Give rapid attempt-specific feedback through the existing scaffolded-independence protocol.
- After supported success, verify with a fresh unassisted item.
- Do not infer a stable bottleneck from one ambiguous error.

## 5. Interleaving and discrimination
Use interleaving after the learner has enough foundation to distinguish between related methods, concepts, representations, or problem types.

- Mix confusable or competing approaches when the learning objective includes choosing which one applies.
- Do not label the required method in the stem when method selection is what is being tested. Ask the learner to select or justify the strategy before or while executing it.
- Prefer meaningful contrasts (for example BFS vs DFS, stack vs queue, precision vs recall trade-offs) over random topic switching.
- Introduce a new technique with enough focused practice first; premature interleaving must not turn missing instruction into guessing.
- In Review Mode, use discrimination value as a selection factor alongside due recall, gaps, uncertainty, and coverage.

## Boundaries
Active retrieval, spacing, error diagnosis, progressive hints, and independent evidence already have dedicated Tutor me policies; do not duplicate them here. Focus/time-management systems and knowledge-management systems such as Pomodoro, deep-work scheduling, PARA, Zettelkasten, or evergreen notes are outside Tutor me unless the learner explicitly asks for study-planning advice.
